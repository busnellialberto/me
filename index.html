About

Where data and research meet agri-food, sustainability and biodiversity.

I've spent my career in monitoring engineering at enterprise scale, leading teams on observability, anomaly detection and forecasting across large, mission-critical systems. The work has always come down to one thing: turning noisy, high-volume data into something you can trust and act on before problems surface.

I hold a degree in Computer Engineering from Politecnico di Milano. From there I grew into monitoring engineering, moving from building individual monitoring systems to leading the function: the observability, anomaly detection and automation that keep large-scale services reliable, and the instinct for catching problems before they turn into failures.

I'm now directing that same discipline toward agri-food and sustainability. A farm network is, technically, a problem I know well: vast fleets of field sensors, weather and satellite data streaming in, where the value lies in data quality, anomaly detection and predictive alerts. Early warning on disease, pests or water stress, precision irrigation, yield, traceability. Underneath it runs a long-standing personal passion for botany, wild plants and biodiversity, which makes this not a pivot of convenience but a direction I genuinely care about.
